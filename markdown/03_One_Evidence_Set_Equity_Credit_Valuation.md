# CreditSwan | One set of evidence for equity, credit and valuation
## Breitling at Partners Group

**Partners Group master demo · Markdown edition · 16 October 2026 · Prepared for Andrew Bellis, Head of Private Credit**

> **Illustrative model.** Every Breitling operating, financial, debt and valuation figure in this document is an illustrative model (baseline assumptions A-02, A-09, A-14), not company data. **Breitling's debt quantum, lenders and terms are not public**; only the ratings and an October 2028 maturity for part of the debt are [Public]. The 13.0x multiple is an illustrative calibration to listed luxury and watch peers, **not Partners Group's mark**; the valuation committee sets the real mark. Whether a Partners Group credit vehicle may lend to, or hold debt of, a Partners Group portfolio company is a **cross-side question for the conflicts and compliance process**; this demo shows the evidence, not an allocation. Public facts are tagged [Public]. Amounts in CHF unless stated; TTM to September 2026 unless stated.

**The decision:** connect Breitling's systems once, build one source-traced fact base of 20 facts, and let the board and the deal team, the lenders and CLO holders, and the valuation committee read their own view from it. On 15 October that single fact base caught **CHF 1.9m** of recurring cost booked as non-recurring and told all three teams the same thing in the same hour, before the lender report was sent and before the September NAV was struck. Pilot Breitling for eight weeks, then extend to Aroma-Zone, where Partners Group's years as a lender make one fact base worth the most.

## 02 | Why three teams keep three numbers today

**Three teams pull the same ledger, apply three definitions on three calendars, and then spend days explaining why the answers differ.** None of them is wrong. Each is right for a different document: the operating plan, the credit agreement and the valuation file.

| Team | Number they quote | Definition source | Refresh | Typical lag after month end |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Board and deal team | CHF 171.0m run-rate | Investment committee model | Monthly | ~12 days |
| Valuation committee | CHF 154.3m maintainable | Valuation policy, peer method | Monthly NAV; quarterly committee | ~30 days |
| Lenders and agent | CHF 160.4m covenant | Credit agreement "Consolidated EBITDA" | Monthly or quarterly lender report | ~35 days |
| Evergreen investors | Look-through from the NAV | Fund reporting | Monthly | ~45 days |
| CLO holders | Covenant and rating inputs | Trustee and manager reports | Quarterly | ~60 days |
| Breitling management accounts | CHF 148.6m reported | GL | Monthly | ~10 days |

| Cost of keeping three numbers (illustrative, per company) | Hours per quarter today | With one fact base |
|---|---:|---:|
| Equity: rebuild the monthly pack and run-rate bridge | 55 | 12 |
| Credit: recompute the lender report, refresh the model, chase support | 60 | 15 |
| Valuation: refresh inputs, valuation memo, look-through | 50 | 13 |
| **Total** | **165** | **40** |

| Gap | What it looks like | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Definition gap | CHF 148.6m vs 154.3m vs 160.4m vs 171.0m quoted in the same week | Every meeting starts with a reconciliation |
| Timing gap | The board sees September in October; CLO holders see it in December | A problem visible to one team is invisible to another for weeks |
| Evidence gap | Add-backs accepted from the company's schedule, not the GL and payroll | Errors surface at audit or at a refinancing, not at source |
| Memory gap | Each team keeps its own workpapers | No single answer to "where did this number come from?" |

**The insight:** the four EBITDAs are not a disagreement; they are one fact base read through four documents. The problem is that nobody holds the fact base, so every difference looks like an error, and every real error looks like a difference.

**Assumptions on this tab:** hours and lags are illustrative estimates for a large portfolio company with one credit facility; lags are typical market practice, not Partners Group's own calendar.

|---|---:|---:|---|

**The insight:** every size test passed, which is exactly why the error survived. A CHF 5.7m add-back on a CHF 774m business sits under audit materiality and inside every cap. ## 08 | The September package event

**On Thursday 15 October one restated fact moved all three views, and every team heard about it in its own language within the hour.** Breitling's September package went to the owners and the lenders' agent. CreditSwan recomputed it from the GL, payroll and the HR position register and found CHF 1.9m booked as non-recurring restructuring cost that recurs: recruitment fees, sign-on and retention payments for 14 US and UK boutique roles back-filled within 60 days of the 2026 cost programme.

## 09 | Lineage and audit trail

**Every number in every view can be walked back to a journal line, and every change leaves a footprint.**

| Step | Object | Example for F-07 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GL journal lines | Restructuring cost codes for the 2026 cost programme, September close |
| 2 | Join to payroll | Payments by employee: severance, recruitment fees, sign-on and retention |
| 3 | Join to the HR position register | Position status: exited and closed, or refilled, with dates |
| 4 | Apply definition | Non-recurring means it does not recur; a role refilled within 60 days is a recurring cost |
| 5 | Fact version | F-07 v14: CHF 5.7m (as submitted); F-07 v15: CHF 3.8m (as recomputed) |
| 6 | Exception | EX-0415: CHF 1.9m across 14 US and UK boutique roles, evidence attached |
| 7 | Owner response | Controller confirms back-fill dates against the position register, 10:15 |
| 8 | Sign-off | CFO approves v15, 10:16 |
| 9 | Views recompute | Covenant, maintainable and run-rate EBITDA, lender report lines, valuation inputs |
| 10 | Notifications | One per team, per permission (Tab 11) |

| Audit question | Answer the trail gives |
|---|---|
| Who changed the number? | CreditSwan flagged; Breitling's controller confirmed; the CFO approved |
| What did each team see before? | Frozen view snapshots at v14, kept for each team |
| Was any view edited by hand? | No; views hold formulas over fact IDs, and overrides are logged as exceptions |
| Can an auditor re-perform it? | Yes; the GL extract, payroll extract, position register and definition text are stored with the version |
| Which external documents used v14? | None sent; the draft lender report and the NAV input were replaced before release |

**Assumptions on this tab:** version numbers, exception IDs and times are illustrative.

## 11 | Governance, permissions and information barriers

**One fact base does not mean one audience: the facts are shared, but each team sees only what its role, its contracts and the walls allow.** Partners Group's private equity, private credit and portfolio valuation functions all touch Breitling, and they are not interchangeable.

| Team | Boutique-level operating | Lender report | Valuation inputs | Fact lineage | Other holdings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breitling board and Partners Group deal team | Full | Read | Read | Full | None |
| CVC (co-owner) | Full | Read | Own file | Full | None |
| Lenders and agent | Summary | Full | None | Lender facts | None |
| Valuation committee | Derived | Read | Full | Full | Own book |
| Evergreen investors and wealth clients | None | None | Look-through | None | None |
| Partners Group private credit | None | None | None | None | Own borrowers only |

| Control | How it works |
|---|---|
| Fact layer shared, view layer walled | Facts carry evidence; views carry opinions. Walls sit on views and commentary |
| Lender information flows through the borrower | CreditSwan flags to Breitling; Breitling's CFO issues to the agent. No back channel |
| Cross-side test before any position | A Partners Group credit or CLO position in a Partners Group equity holding goes to conflicts and compliance first, with fund conflict policies and investor disclosures |
| Independent marks | The valuation committee sets the mark; inputs can be re-performed by auditors from the trace |
| Two owners | CVC's view is walled from Partners Group commentary and gets the same facts |
| Logging | Every view, export and alert logged by user; quarterly access review by compliance |

**The insight:** the wall gets stronger, not weaker. Today walls are policed by people choosing not to forward spreadsheets. With one fact base, the wall is a permission on a view, logged and reviewable, and the shared layer holds only facts every party could see in the ordinary course.

**Assumptions on this tab:** permissions are a proposed design (A-19); whether a Partners Group credit vehicle may hold Breitling debt, and on what terms, is for compliance and counsel. Nothing here is legal advice.
