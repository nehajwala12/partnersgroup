Section 03 · Swiss watch market

Swiss watch exports are recovering slowly from the 2024 peak-to-trough, but the US, Breitling's largest market, is still moving month to month on tariffs and price

This tab reads the market for an owner and a lender: exports and demand, the US tariff shock, price tiers and the competitive set, the pre-owned market as an outside signal, and the sector risks a lender should watch. Public figures carry their source and date; CreditSwan estimates are labelled.

Swiss watch exports fell in 2024 for the first time since the pandemic, and 2026 has only just turned positive year to date

Exports peaked at CHF 26.7bn in 2023 and slipped 2.8% in 2024. By August 2026 the year-to-date total was CHF 17.24bn, up 1.7%, the first positive reading of the year, with August itself up 9.1%.

The US tariff on Swiss goods went from 10% to 39% and back to 15% inside eight months, and exports to the US swung with it

A 39% rate applied from August 2025; a deal cut it to 15% in November 2025. Exports to the US fell 24% in August 2025 and 55.6% in September as brands paused shipments, rebounded in July 2026 and fell again in August. For a brand whose largest market is the US, sell-in no longer tells you what consumers bought.

Breitling sits in the middle of luxury, the tier squeezed between brands with waiting lists and brands competing on price

Exports in the CHF 500–3,000 export-price band fell 0.9% in August 2026 while the total grew 9.1%. In retail terms that band covers most of Breitling's core range, alongside Omega, TAG Heuer, Tudor and IWC.

The pre-owned market is the fastest outside read of demand, and it has been softening for Breitling's core references

Listings and asking prices on licensed pre-owned panels move weekly. A falling price index with rising listings usually means grey-market supply from dealers, which shows up in the brand's own numbers a quarter later.

A strong franc cut Breitling's reported sales by about a tenth in FY26 without a single fewer watch being sold

The franc rose more than 10% against the dollar over the year to March 2026. With costs in francs and the largest market in dollars, translation and margin both suffer, which is why the board reads demand in local currency.

Listed peers trade at a range of multiples, and the valuation in Section 07 sits inside it

The valuation view uses a 13.0x maintainable EBITDA multiple, inside the range implied by listed watch and luxury peers. The multiple is an assumption, not a valuation opinion, and the register says how much it moves the answer.

Section 04 · Breitling

Breitling earns most of its profit in mature boutiques and core dealers; the newer boutiques, the US channel and inventory decide the next two years

This tab is the diligence read of the company: channels, regions, the boutique network and its vintages, the brands, costs and cash, and the debt. Public facts are tagged; every operating figure is an illustrative model calibrated to FY26 sales of CHF 769m and adjusted EBITDA of CHF 162m.

Direct-to-consumer is 46% of sales in the model and carries the higher margin, but wholesale still decides where volume goes

Company boutiques and e-commerce bring in CHF 356m; authorised dealers and partner boutiques CHF 418m. DTC earns the full retail margin and owns the price; wholesale carries less fixed cost but needs sell-out data to be read properly.

The Americas are the largest region and the most volatile; Asia-Pacific is smaller but recovering

The US alone is CHF 209m, 27% of sales. Europe is steadier and the UK is growing again; China is recovering from a low base.

Boutiques opened before 2020 make three-quarters of boutique profit; those opened since 2025 are still losing money

The 102 company boutiques earn CHF 84.4m at the four walls. The 48 opened before 2020 earn 31%; the 12 opened since 2025 are at −9% and 22% behind their sales plan. Moody's cited the fixed cost of store expansion in its 2025 downgrade.

Universal Genève and Gallet add growth options at different price points, and both cost marketing before they earn

The House of Brands structure gives each brand its own MD. In the model, launch costs of about CHF 3.0m a year sit in reported EBITDA and are normalised only in the owner's run-rate view.

Inventory is the cash question: 318 days of cost of sales ties up about CHF 229m

Gross margin of 66% is strong, but stock builds ahead of the holiday season and slow US sell-through have pushed inventory days above 300. The 2026 cost programme removed more than 50 roles; the model credits CHF 6.1m of run-rate savings.

Leverage is about six times covenant EBITDA in the model, with a springing test that only bites if the revolver is drawn

The structure here is illustrative: Breitling's debt quantum and terms are not public. What is public is the rating path (B- at S&P since July 2025, a Moody's downgrade in 2025), Bloomberg's report that the loans sit in the lowest tier of high yield, and that part of the debt matures in October 2028.

US dealers took September stock ahead of the October price rise faster than they sold it

Sell-in to US dealers ran at 1.42 times sell-out in September, against 1.0–1.1 in a normal month. Six dealer groups account for 80% of the excess. That is revenue booked early, not lost, but it sets up a weak Q4 for sell-in.

Realised prices are drifting down and pre-owned prices have fallen through the floor

The realised discount against list has widened by about ten basis points a week. The pre-owned index for core references fell below 74% in W37 and is at 71%: the outside market is telling the board that dealer stock is leaking.

The boutiques opened since 2025 keep slipping behind plan, while the mature network grows like for like

The 12 newest boutiques are at 78% of their sales plan, down from 86% in July. Four sites account for most of the gap. Like-for-like sales in the rest of the network are up 1.9%.

Liquidity is comfortable, but inventory and the revolver are the two lines a lender reads first

Liquidity of CHF 197m is almost double the CHF 100m floor. Inventory at 318 days is above the limit, and the revolver at 30% drawn is ten points from the level that springs the leverage test.

Every breach opens an exception with a named owner, and a failed feed freezes its monitors rather than guessing

Five exceptions are open or recently closed. Four of six feeds pass their quality gate; dealer sell-out reports from four groups still arrive monthly rather than weekly.

Section 06 · Value plan

Seven fixes in the existing network lift run-rate EBITDA from CHF 160m to about CHF 184m, and a boutique gate keeps new capex behind the evidence

This tab sizes the levers, tests the boutique formats against each other, lays out the 100-day plan and the pilots, and shows what the plan does for the 2028 refinancing and an exit. Every figure is illustrative and recomputed in code from the levers.

Seven levers add about CHF 23.5m of run-rate EBITDA without opening a boutique

The largest is getting the boutiques opened since 2025 to plan; the fastest are discount discipline and boutique labour. Each lever is a board monitor with a target, so progress is read weekly rather than reported quarterly.

Half the levers delivered still adds CHF 12m; none delivered leaves the plan dependent on the market

The board reads the levers in three cases. Even the half case takes leverage below 5.7x before the 2028 maturity window.

A company boutique costs four to twelve times a partner boutique and returns about a third as much

A standard company boutique costs about CHF 3.5m and a flagship relocation about CHF 9.5m; a partner boutique needs about CHF 0.8m of fit-out contribution and stock. On year-3 cash, partner formats return about 42% against 11–16%. The gate holds company openings until the newest cohort reaches 90% of plan.

The 100-day plan is six workstreams, each measured by board monitors rather than slides

Every workstream has an owner, a monitor and a day-100 target. The board reads them every Monday, and the lender report draws from the same numbers.

Seven pilots, each read as treatment minus control, and two of them are not working

A board with no losing pilots is not measuring anything. Corporate gifting in the US and a Gallet pop-up programme read negative or inside the noise and are reported as such.

The same evidence that runs the plan is the record a lender or an IPO investor will ask for

Part of the debt matures in October 2028 and an IPO has been discussed for 2027–29. Both need maintainable earnings, clean of one-offs, with a source trail. The evidence set builds that record every week rather than once, at the end.

Section 07 · One evidence set

One set of facts from Breitling's systems serves the board, the lenders and the valuation committee, and one restated fact moved all three before 10:30

Today each team keeps its own version of the company and learns about the same problem weeks apart. Here one connection to Breitling's systems produces twenty versioned facts, each traced to its rows, and three views compute from them. Everything on this tab is an illustrative model of how it works on a Partners Group holding.

Twenty facts, each with one system of record, one owner and one sign-off

No team keeps its own copy. Each view is a set of formulas over fact IDs, and each fact walks back to its source rows. A fact with two candidate sources is split until each has one.

At 09:40 CreditSwan found CHF 1.9m booked as non-recurring that recurs; by 10:30 the lender report, the equity story and the valuation input had all moved

Breitling's September package went to the owners and the lenders' agent at 09:00. Joining the GL restructuring codes to payroll and the HR position register showed that recruitment fees, sign-on and retention payments for 14 back-filled US and UK boutique roles had been booked as restructuring. The definition allows non-recurring items only.

For the board and the deal team the restatement trims run-rate EBITDA to CHF 169.1m and shows that a third of the cost programme's boutique savings have been hired back

Maintainable earnings, clean of one-offs, are what an IPO investor will price. Catching the re-hiring now keeps it out of the equity story and points the boutique-labour lever at the right sites.

For the lenders the report is corrected before it is sent: covenant EBITDA CHF 158.5m, net leverage 6.14x against the 8.00x springing level

The revolver is 30% drawn, so the leverage test is not live; the lender report and the CLO managers' questions are. The same view runs on every borrower in Partners Group's own credit book.

For the valuation committee the maintainable EBITDA input falls CHF 1.9m, equity value 2.4%, and the change is in the file before the September NAV is struck

At 13.0x, enterprise value moves CHF 24.7m and Partners Group's share about CHF 12.6m, roughly three basis points at the level of the evergreen programs in the illustration. The size is small; the point is that every input to the mark now carries its source rows, its version and its approver.

One fact base does not mean one audience: each team sees its own view, and nothing it should not

Private-side information stays with its team through Microsoft 365 permissions and sensitivity labels. Every view and change is logged. Any Partners Group credit or CLO position in Breitling's own debt is flagged to compliance first, never assumed.

The same pattern fits the other Partners Group holdings where weekly evidence matters most

Each company gets its own fact base with the same three views. A business that Partners Group has lent to and now proposes to own is the clearest case: the credit file becomes the first draft of the equity diligence.
